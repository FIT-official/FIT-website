The physics

Vector position: r(t) = x(t)i + y(t)j, in metres.

Velocity: v(t) = dr/dt. Its components are dx/dt and dy/dt, in m/s.

Acceleration: a(t) = dv/dt, in m/s².

Speed: |v| = √(vₓ² + vᵧ²), in m/s.

Circular motion: normal acceleration magnitude aₙ = v²/r, towards the centre.

Non-uniform circular motion: total acceleration magnitude |a| = √(aₜ² + aₙ²), because tangential and normal acceleration are perpendicular.

Angular motion: ω = v/r. With constant tangential acceleration aₜ, angular acceleration α = aₜ/r and θ(t) = θ₀ + ω₀t + ½αt².

Relative velocity: v_ground = v_boat/water + v_water/ground, added as vectors.

River crossing: take east as +x and the far bank as +y. With heading θ measured eastward from north, vₓ = U sinθ + current and vᵧ = U cosθ.

Crossing time: T = river width / vᵧ for positive vᵧ.

All examples use idealised point objects. Circular trajectories have fixed radius; river current and boat speed relative to water are constant.
