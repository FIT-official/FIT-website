The chemistry

Ideal gas: pV = nRT. For university calculations use R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ and T(K) = θ(°C) + 273.15.

van der Waals equation: (p + an²/V²)(V − nb) = nRT, so p = nRT/(V − nb) − an²/V².

Constants: a has units Pa m⁶ mol⁻² and b has units m³ mol⁻¹. The parameters in each challenge are supplied for the named gas.

Compressibility: Z = pV/(nRT). Ideal gases have Z = 1. Values below 1 indicate attractive effects dominate the departure from ideality; values above 1 indicate repulsive or excluded-volume effects dominate.

Maxwell–Boltzmann speed density: f(v) = 4π(m/(2πkT))³ᐟ² v² exp(−mv²/(2kT)), where m is the mass of one molecule.

Speeds: v_mp = √(2kT/m), v_mean = √(8kT/(πm)), v_rms = √(3kT/m).

Constants: k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ and N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Molecular mass = molar mass/N_A.

Conventions: work done BY the gas is positive on expansion and negative on compression. Reversible boundary work is W_by = ∫p dV. No cyclic processes are included.

Assumptions: single-component gas in thermal equilibrium; classical, non-relativistic molecules; van der Waals equation used as an approximate single-phase equation of state; no chemical reactions. Calculations require T above 0 K, V above 0, and V greater than nb.
