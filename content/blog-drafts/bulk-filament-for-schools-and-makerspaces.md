# Bulk Filament for Schools and Makerspaces: How to Plan and Order

A school lab, CCA or makerspace gets through filament quickly. One class project can use several spools, and running out halfway through a term is frustrating for everyone. Buying in bulk makes sense, but only if you buy the right materials and colours.

This guide covers how to estimate what you need, which materials suit group work, and how our bulk filament enquiry works.

## Step 1: Work out what you will print

Start with the projects, not the spools. List the main activities for the term or year: keychains, name tags, phone stands, room models, robot parts or prototypes. For each one, note:

- roughly how many students or teams will print;
- how many prints each will make, including failed prints and reprints;
- the colours you actually need.

Your slicer shows the filament weight for each print. Multiply that by the number of prints, add a margin for failures and testing, and divide by the spool weight (1kg) to get a rough number of rolls. It is better to be slightly over than to run short in the week of a deadline. [TO CONFIRM: whether FIT wants to suggest a specific safety margin.]

## Step 2: Choose materials that suit group work

For most school and makerspace printing, **PLA** is the sensible default. It is easy to print, holds detail well and does not need an enclosure. It is the material we recommend for first prints.

Add **PETG** if students are making functional parts that need to be tougher or will be used outdoors or in damp places. PETG offers greater strength, durability and temperature and chemical resistance than PLA, but it can string more and grip the plate strongly, so it suits slightly more experienced users.

For display work, **Marble PLA** and **Wood PLA** add a stone-like or wood-grain finish without painting, and they print like standard PLA.

Our [filament types guide](https://www.fixitoday.com/blog/3d-printing-filament-types-guide) compares the materials in more detail.

## Step 3: Pick colours with a purpose

Colour choice matters more than it seems in a classroom:

- **Neutral colours** (white, black, grey) work for prototypes and parts that will be painted.
- **A few bright colours** help teams tell their parts apart.
- **Transparent or light colours** are useful for light-up projects with LEDs.

## Storing filament in a shared space

Bulk filament only stays useful if it stays dry. In a shared lab:

- keep unopened spools sealed in their bags;
- store opened spools in airtight boxes with desiccant;
- label boxes by material and colour so students return spools to the right place;
- dry spools that have been left out before use. We list a [filament dehydrator](https://www.fixitoday.com/products/3d-printing-filament-dehydrator-4-level-220v-350w) at SGD 35.20.

## Tips for teachers and makerspace leads

- **Standardise.** Using one PLA brand across all printers makes slicer profiles simpler.
- **Keep a log.** Note which class used which spool. It makes the next order easier to estimate.
- **Plan for failed prints.** First-time users will have some. That is part of learning.
- **Check printers before term starts.** A clogged nozzle or worn part can waste filament. If a printer needs attention, email us with the model, fault, photos and a short video.
