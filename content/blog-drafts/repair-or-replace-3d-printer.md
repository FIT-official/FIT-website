# Repair or Replace Your 3D Printer? A Practical Guide

When a 3D printer stops working properly, the first instinct is often to look at new models. Sometimes that is the right call. Often, though, the fault is a worn part, a blocked nozzle or a calibration problem that can be fixed for much less than a new machine.

This guide explains how to think about repair versus replacement, what to check yourself first, and how to send us a repair enquiry that gets you a clear answer quickly.

## Start with the symptom, not the guess

A repair starts with the fault, not a guess. Before deciding anything, write down exactly what is happening:

- What were you printing, and with which filament?
- When did the problem start? Suddenly, or gradually?
- Is there an error message on the screen or in the app?
- Does it happen on every print, or only some?

Take photos and a short video of the problem. A ten-second clip of a nozzle that is not extruding, or a noise from the motion system, tells a technician a great deal.

## Common faults we see

We repair Bambu Lab, Prusa, Creality and other FDM printers, subject to the model and fault. The problems we handle include:

- **Nozzle or extruder faults** — under-extrusion, clicking, or no filament coming out.
- **Filament path or AMS faults** — filament failing to load, retract or feed.
- **Bed adhesion and first-layer problems** — prints lifting or not sticking.
- **Motion faults** — layer shifts, grinding or unusual noises.
- **Heating faults** — the hotend or bed not reaching or holding temperature.
- **Print-quality problems** — stringing, rough surfaces or dimensional errors.

Our [common 3D printer problems](https://www.fixitoday.com/blog/common-3d-printer-problems) guide covers many of these in more detail.

## What to check yourself first

Some problems are quick to fix at home:

1. **Filament condition.** Damp filament causes popping, stringing and weak layers. Try a fresh or dried spool. A [filament dehydrator](https://www.fixitoday.com/products/3d-printing-filament-dehydrator-4-level-220v-350w) helps if this is a regular problem.
2. **Build plate.** Clean it as the manufacturer recommends, and check you are using the right plate and settings for the material.
3. **Nozzle.** Partial clogs are common. For Bambu Lab X1 Carbon owners, our [nozzle clog troubleshooting guide](https://www.fixitoday.com/blog/bambu-x1-carbon-nozzle-clog-troubleshooting) walks through the checks.
4. **Feed path.** On AMS machines, look for tangled spools or a worn tube. See our [AMS feed troubleshooting guide](https://www.fixitoday.com/blog/bambu-ams-filament-feed-troubleshooting).
5. **Firmware and slicer profile.** Make sure you are on the expected firmware and have selected the right filament profile.

If the problem persists after these checks, or if you are not comfortable opening up the printer, it is time for a professional look.

## When repair makes sense

Repair is usually worth it when:

- the printer has been reliable until recently;
- the fault points to a single part, such as a nozzle or hotend;
- the printer still meets your needs for size, speed and materials;
- replacement parts are available for your model.

Regular maintenance — cleaning, calibration and replacing worn parts — also keeps a printer going for longer and avoids bigger faults.

## When replacement may make more sense

Replacing the printer may be the better choice when:

- several major parts have failed at once and the combined cost approaches the price of a new machine;
- parts for your model are hard to find;
- you have outgrown the printer and need a larger build volume or multi-material printing.
