# Repair photo storage and decoding

Repair photos use the existing private MongoDB connection. This avoids the missing private S3 bucket configuration without using the public product bucket or changing storage permissions. Existing private fabrication photos remain readable through their original verified S3 path. No migration or deletion is performed.

An upload requires Clerk identity, a same-origin request, the existing asset rate limit, a single bounded multipart file, and successful decoding. Maximum input is 3 MiB and 24 million pixels, with either dimension at most 12,000. JPEG, PNG, WebP and single-image HEIC/HEIF are supported. Animated images, sequences, forged signatures and malformed files are rejected. Output is metadata-stripped JPEG, at most 2048 pixels on either side and 512 KiB. EXIF orientation is applied where available; HEIC decoding applies the HEIF transform through libheif. A filename alone is never evidence that an image decoded.

Database reservations cap retained JPEG bytes at 25 MiB globally, 5 MiB per account, 15 uploads per account per UTC day and 2,000 images globally. Atomic counters prevent concurrent oversubscription; a failed or uncertain save may conservatively consume a reservation. These are technical capacity limits, not pricing, stock, retention or service promises. Original photos are not auto-deleted. Capacity exhaustion keeps the user's draft and explains the option to contact FIT or send without photos. Separate future maintenance must review unused reservations and retained records explicitly.

The existing M0 cluster has a documented 0.5 GB logical storage limit (uncompressed documents plus indexes) and 500 connections: https://www.mongodb.com/docs/atlas/reference/free-shared-limitations/ . A read-only check on 2026-10-11 at 05:11 UTC found this application's dataSize 11,388,355 bytes and indexSize 2,244,608 bytes. This is one database snapshot, not a cluster-wide free-space guarantee or evidence that the connection incident recovered. The small retained-image cap bounds this feature's contribution. The feature shares the application pool; it does not create a new Mongo client per photo.

Photos are accessible only to their owner or an administrator reviewing a saved linked assessment. Responses are private/no-store and cannot be embedded cross-origin. Email attaches all selected photos as JPEGs to the existing verified FIT owner address; failure to read any photo prevents partial delivery and is recorded for explicit bounded retry. Ambiguous SMTP results are never retried automatically.

## Decoder provenance

The exact server-only dependency is libheif-js 1.23.5 (https://github.com/catdad-experiments/libheif-js), distributed under LGPL-3.0; its LICENSE and source/build references are included in node_modules/libheif-js. The package and bundled decoder are unmodified, replaceable server dependencies; no decoder is shipped to the browser. Upstream source: https://github.com/strukturag/libheif . The application wrapper is lib/printerRepair/photoDecodeWorker.cjs. Each conversion uses a fresh worker with an eight-second deadline, bounded declared image dimensions before pixel allocation, and termination after completion. Worker resourceLimits constrain the JavaScript heap; they are not a whole-process/native-memory guarantee.

Next output tracing must include the worker and complete libheif-js package. Verify the built upload route trace before publishing. Synthetic fixtures and generation details are in tests/fixtures/repair-photos; they contain no customer images. Functional phone tests use a phone-sized Chromium viewport; physical iOS/Safari camera and production Clerk sessions require separate acceptance.
